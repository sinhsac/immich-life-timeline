Storytelling

By default the duration is an outcome, not a request: chapters with more photos get more screen time, and a longer journey gets more chapters. Untick this to force a number.

The photo count follows from the duration, not the other way around. Every chapter always gets at least one photo so no stretch of time disappears, and each chapter's highest-scoring photo is held longer as the hero shot.

Head pose

EAR stays a hard gate either way — a photo with the eyes shut cannot be rescued by any amount of sharpness.

Hard limits — where a photo is rejected outright

With scoring on, the sliders above are only knees. These are the walls. The default used to cut at 22° of head turn, and 22° is still very nearly looking straight at the lens: photos of someone grinning back over their shoulder, mid-run, blowing out candles were thrown away before they were ever scored, and what survived was passport shots.

The server keeps every wall on the far side of its own knee, so an inconsistent pair is corrected rather than inverting the score.

Image quality

Smiling

Everything else here measures a technically clean face; nothing measured whether the moment was worth keeping. A smile is the closest this metric set gets to "a moment", and it is the largest single bonus in the score — a photo turned 35° with a broad smile now outscores a dead-straight, unsmiling one.

Leave at 0. A solemn portrait is part of the story too, so this is off by default and the preference is applied by adding points rather than by rejecting.

Duplicate shots

Bursts collapse to their best frame. 0 turns it off. A burst is capped at three times this figure, so a whole afternoon of photos taken 5 s apart does not shrink to a single frame. Video clips are never collapsed — they all share the video's timestamp, and the indexer already guaranteed they do not overlap.

Video clips

The indexer job scans every video frame, finds the person you selected, then cuts out the best clip. Any chapter that has a clip gets one slot for it — a moving clip is worth more than a photo that is only marginally better.

Measured in "face widths per second" — divided by face size, not frame size, because a large face moving 50 px is normal while a small face moving 50 px is a jump cut. High accepts shaky handheld footage, low keeps only genuinely steady clips.

Framing

Small keeps the whole person and the surroundings, large turns it into a tight portrait. The face is only the anchor that stops the video from jumping.

Lower it to show more headroom, raise it to show more of the body.

Advanced framing

Only used on photos holding two of the selected people. The two eye centres are not treated as one pair of eyes — a tall parent and a short child are 30° apart and levelling that wrecks the photo. Instead two virtual points are placed on a horizontal line the real distance apart, so the further apart they stand the wider the framing opens. Photos with only one of them still anchor normally on that one face.

Lower is better looking and larger. 20 is visually clean for this material.

Editing style

The zoom is centred on the midpoint between the eyes, so the face stays in exactly one place — only the surrounding context widens and narrows.

Audio from video clips

Audio comes in before the picture and stays on after the picture has cut away — the ear hears the new space before the eye sees it, and it does not stop dead at the same instant as the picture. Between clips there is silence, because still photos have no sound.

Fades and level

A fade is never longer than half its own clip, so these are ceilings rather than exact figures.

Background music

Photos are silent, video clips are not. Two clips separated by four photos means five seconds of silence and then sound slamming back in. The audio lead and tail soften the two edges; they cannot fill the hole in between. Music is what holds a continuous bed of sound under the whole thing — a structural fix, not decoration.

Tracks are read from the MUSIC_DIR directory on the server, mounted read-only. There is deliberately no upload: the name ends up on an ffmpeg command line, so every check lives in one place and nothing writes into that directory.

Ducking is computed, not compressed: the exact seconds where real audio plays are already known before ffmpeg is called, so the envelope is exact and identical between runs.

This does not replace the hero/chapter structure, it changes the unit: each shot keeps its natural length as the target and the real boundary is pulled to the nearest beat. Expect the duration to drift a little from the estimate — that is inherent to snapping. Video clips are only ever snapped down, never up, because snapping up would freeze the last frame mid-motion.

At 140 BPM a single beat is 0.43 s, too fast for a memory video — try 2. Beat detection can also report half the real tempo, which this compensates for.

Advanced timing

A story needs room to be entered and room to be let go of.

The title sits on top of the first photo, not on its own black screen. If the first shot is a video clip it is not extended for the card either — that would show a frozen frame before the clip starts moving.

0 means hard cuts, which is a real choice and not the same thing as leaving it unset. A cross-fade never lengthens the video: it overlaps the tail of the shot before it.
